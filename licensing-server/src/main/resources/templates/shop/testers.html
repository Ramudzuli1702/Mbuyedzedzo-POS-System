Before you start

You'll be testing with a real licence key, not a trial, so the first thing to do is request one below. It only takes a couple of minutes to fill in, and the key usually arrives by email within one business day.

Getting set up

  1. Request a licence. Go to Pricing, pick the edition you'll be testing, and click "Request" on whichever licence type you've been asked to test. Fill in your name, a business name (anything's fine for testing), and your email. A licence key and a login for managing it will land in your inbox.
  2. Download. Once your key arrives, go to Download and pick the matching edition. It's a Windows installer, about 250 MB.
  3. Install. Run the .exe and follow the prompts. Windows will ask for permission to make changes to your PC, say yes: the installer sets up its own private database behind the scenes. The very first launch can take a few minutes while that finishes, which is normal, not a freeze. If antivirus or SmartScreen flags the installer as new and unrecognised, choose "More info" → "Run anyway".
  4. Activate. On first launch, paste in the licence key from your email and click Activate.
  5. First login. The very first account you create becomes the admin account for that installation. Use it for your own testing; if you want to see what a cashier sees, create a second, more limited account later from User Management.
What's inside

A quick tour of each part

You'll find all of these down the left-hand menu once you're signed in. The app also offers a short "get to know the system" walkthrough the first time it opens, and you can come back to the same explanations any time from the small ⓘ About this screen button at the top of any page.

Sales

The till itself. Scan or search a product, build up a cart, take payment, print or email the receipt.

Inventory

Add products, track stock, print barcodes. Each product has both a purchase price and a selling price.

User Management

Add staff accounts and set what each person is allowed to do.

Customers

Keep contact details and purchase history for regulars (Standard edition only).

Reports

Revenue, real profit margin, best sellers, stock health. Export anything to Excel.

Sessions

A log of who was logged in, when, and for how long.

Approvals

Refunds and exchanges above a set amount wait here for a manager to approve.

Marketing

Promo codes and discounts, and how well each one performs (Standard edition only).

Subscription

Licence status, renewal date, and billing.

Settings

Business details, receipt layout, tax rate, printer and other preferences.

Scanning without an Android phone

There's a companion Android app that turns a phone into a wireless barcode scanner. If you're on an iPhone, you can't use that specific app today: an iOS version would need to be built on a Mac, which isn't part of this round of testing.

That doesn't stop you from testing the real thing, though. Every barcode field also accepts just typing the number in, so you can ring up a product without scanning anything. And a cheap wired USB barcode scanner (under R300 at most electronics shops) plugs in and works with no setup, no drivers, and no phone at all, arguably the better test of the real checkout experience anyway.

Please do this

Document what you see

While you're testing, take photos or screen recordings of yourself using the system, ringing up a sale, adding stock, pulling up a report, whatever you're doing at the time. Seeing exactly what you saw and clicked is far more useful to us than a written description alone, especially for anything that looked wrong, felt slow, or confused you.

A phone video of the screen while you talk through what you're doing is ideal. It doesn't need to be polished, rough and honest is exactly what we want. Send these along with your written feedback below.

Telling us what you find

Use it the way you'd actually run a shop day: ring up sales, add stock, issue a refund, pull up a report. Anything that confused you, felt slow, looked wrong, or didn't work the way you expected is exactly the kind of feedback that's useful, even if it feels minor.
